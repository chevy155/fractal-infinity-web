HIGH CONFIDENCE · 86%

Current reality
The ranking does not say frontend wafers, GPU architecture, or HBM design is what currently stops 5× accelerator shipments. It says CoWoS-class packaging is. TSMC’s CEO on the 2Q26 call said packaging capacity is so tight it limits customers’ growth, and that backend is still in shortage mode with a bigger gap — stronger than the 3Q25 “very tight / working to narrow the gap” language. HBM4 is close behind: SK hynix, Micron, and Samsung are all in volume, and SK’s own 2Q26 release still says customer demand exceeds supply capabilities. Capacity-expansion stories (WPM estimates, Arizona AP, Yongin, 13 Taiwan fabs) are not 2026 surplus.
Constraint migration
The engine relieves CoWoS capacity, lead time, and concentration only, then re-ranks. HBM4 rises next because Vera Rubin-class platforms are designed around it and memory makers still report allocation, not idle cubes. After HBM4, the advanced interposer inherits the same physical stack — a CoWoS “slot” without an interposer does not complete the package. HBM3E remains the volume workhorse during the transition and sits just behind the interposer.

CoWoS (80) → HBM4 (71) · engine cascade

Who gains leverage
  • TSMC CoWoS / advanced-packaging tool install rate and Taiwan AP campuses — Arizona packaging is announced, not 2026 deliverable
  • HBM4 makers that can actually ship qualified cubes on time (SK hynix, Micron, Samsung) and the stacking sites behind them (M15X, P&T7, Indiana)
  • Hybrid-bonding equipment (Besi / Applied Materials class) as the shared tool layer under both HBM stacking and SoIC
  • Substrate vendors TSMC is working with — glass-core is ~1 year from production per Wei, so organic high-layer substrates still bind large packages
Who loses leverage
  • The story that frontend wafer starts or GPU architecture is the binding 5× constraint
  • Treating Intel EMIB-T or OSAT spillover as a drop-in for current NVIDIA-class CoWoS — Wei welcomes it as flexibility to unstick frontend wafers, not as equivalence
  • 2027–2028 fab announcements treated as 2026 shippable capacity
  • CPO / silicon photonics as the current accelerator bottleneck — those bind networking scale-out later, not today’s HBM attach
Second-order effect
Clearing CoWoS slots moves the bind onto HBM4 cubes, not onto “more GPUs.” Three-supplier HBM4 is not slack: SK says demand exceeds supply, and HBM wafer intensity also pulls conventional DRAM. The coupled trap: HBM stacking and SoIC both consume hybrid bonders, so HBM relief and denser 3D packaging compete for the same tools. Glass substrates are a cost/size path, not a 2026 bypass.
Opportunity
Worth investigating: who actually holds incremental CoWoS-L allocation into 2027; whether HBM4 16H qualification (still sampling) raises bits-per-placement faster than new cube capacity; whether P&T7 / Indiana packaging relieves stacking faster than wafer starts; and whether EMIB-T takes only non-flagship AI packages. Not a conclusion: TSMC, SK hynix, or Micron as securities, or that the packaging shortage is over.
Watch signals — confirm
  • TSMC continues to describe backend / CoWoS as shortage or as limiting customer growth on subsequent calls
  • SK hynix, Micron, or Samsung keep demand-exceeds-supply, sold-out, or LTA-allocation language
  • Official TSMC WPM is still not disclosed, and secondary capacity figures remain below implied demand
  • HBM4 16H stays in sample / qualification rather than volume
Watch signals — invalidate
  • TSMC states CoWoS supply meets demand with spare allocation and short lead times
  • All three HBM makers report unused HBM capacity and normalized lead times
  • A leading AI flagship package qualifies a true drop-in non-CoWoS path at volume
  • Interposer or substrate lines sit idle while CoWoS tools wait — that would invert the stack
Confidence
Matches the CoWoS-node evidence confidence after the 2Q26 transcript. Direction (packaging over HBM4 over interposer) is better supported than the exact score margin. WPM remains unofficial; glass-substrate timing is a CEO estimate, not a dated process-of-record.
