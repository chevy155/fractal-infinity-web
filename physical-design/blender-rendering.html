Turbine Module V001 — Held in the Field.

Seven solid parts, born in SolidWorks and bound by mates: a Ø160 mm housing, a 12-vane stator, a 16-blade rotor pitched at 62°, a stainless shaft, two bronze bushings, and an end cover. Exported as true solid geometry—no proxy meshes, no sculpted stand-ins—then handed to an ASI-style containment field in Blender.

The field takes the module apart in mid-air, tags every component with its spec, then pulls it back together and drives the rotor up to speed. A hologram you could machine: the same solids generate the GD&T production drawings below.

  • 7 SolidWorks solids
  • 16-blade rotor · 62.2° pitch
  • 12-vane stator
  • Ø160 × 180 mm envelope
  • 7 GD&T drawings

Hold it yourself.

Drag to orbit · scroll or pinch to zoom · the housing is rendered translucent so the stator and rotor stay visible.

From the same CAD: ASME Y14.5 drawings with datums, feature control frames, fits, and angle callouts—generated through the SolidWorks API.

Precision, Set in Motion.

Thirty-one components. Eighteen editable parts. One coordinated grip. Built in SolidWorks, this kinematic prototype joins separate parts through mechanical mates, turning pinion rotation into synchronized jaw movement across a 50 mm opening. Blender reveals the mechanism through cinematic close-ups and an illustrative exploded assembly.

Verified workflow: SolidWorks mate-solved motion exported to Blender for cinematic rendering. This prototype does not claim validated load capacity.
